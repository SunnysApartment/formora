# Reference reel observations

Five Facebook Reels supplied as inspiration, viewed 2026-11-24 by sampling four to six
still frames from each (no audio, not every frame). All five are from the same creator and
carry the caption "From photo to the final logo process". Treat them as inspiration, not
rules, and never reproduce the marks shown.

## What each reel shows

| Reel | Subject photo | Construction seen | Result |
|------|---------------|-------------------|--------|
| 01 | Dog in a stretching pose | Overlapping ellipses on torso, hips, head, paws | Solid one-colour dog silhouette above a sans-serif wordmark |
| 02 | Light aircraft, banking | Straight guide lines along wing, tail and fuselage edges | Solid aircraft silhouette of straight-edged shapes above a wordmark |
| 03 | Cat walking, tail curled up | Many overlapping circles; the tail curl is one circle; a ground line | Solid cat silhouette; the eye and the far legs are thin cut-outs |
| 04 | Elephant walking | Circles for body, head, ear and trunk curve; ground line | Solid elephant silhouette; ear, tusk and leg separation are cut-outs |
| 05 | Ferret walking | Ellipses for tail and body, straight lines for face and legs; vector-editor toolbar visible | Solid ferret silhouette; face mask and legs are cut-outs |

## The shared method

1. **Start from a real pose.** A photo with a clear, characteristic side-on pose. The pose
   carries the personality (stretch, bank, prowl), so choose it before drawing anything.
2. **Lay geometry over the anatomy.** Circles and ellipses for every curve, straight lines
   for every hard edge, one horizontal ground line for the feet.
3. **Keep only the arcs you need.** The outline is traced along segments of those circles
   and lines, so every curve in the mark is part of a true circle or ellipse.
4. **Fill it solid.** One colour, no outline strokes, no shading.
5. **Cut detail back in as negative space.** Eye, ear, tusk, the gap between near and far
   legs: thin cut-outs in the solid shape, not added lines.
6. **Add the wordmark last**, set plainly beneath, in a weight that matches the symbol.

## What to take from it

- This is a **construction** method, not a concept method. Use it in workflow step 4 (the
  construction) and when building the SVG, whenever a concept contains an animal or object
  silhouette. It pairs with Symbolic Geometry and Silhouette Integration in
  `conceptual-techniques.md` and with the path rules in `svg-construction.md`.
- With no photo to work from, describe the pose in words first, then block it in with a
  handful of circles and lines before refining.
- Fewer, larger circles give a calmer mark. If a curve needs more than one arc, simplify
  the pose.
- A few cut-outs separate overlapping limbs better than an outline does, and they survive
  small sizes if the gaps are wide enough.

## What the reels do not show

None of the five fuses a letter with the subject, hides a second meaning in negative
space, or builds the image from typography. Each is a single-subject silhouette with a
separate wordmark beneath, which on its own FORMORA would reject as "icon beside a word".
The fusion idea must still come from the Concept Engine; the reels only show how to draw
the animal or object half cleanly.
